Finding 1 — The 25D Residential Credit Has Gone
Please verify this with your own CPA before acting on it — it is that important
Section 25D terminated for systems placed in service after 31 Dec 2025 this was the 30% credit a homeowner claimed on a cash or loan purchase Section 48E continues — the commercial / third-party-owned credit reaches lease and power purchase agreement structures

For a residential solar company starting up in 2026, this is the single biggest fact in the market. The pitch that carried this industry for a decade — “the government pays for 30% of it” — is no longer available on a straightforward cash or financed sale to a homeowner.

What still works is the third-party-owned route. Where the system is owned by a leasing company or a PPA provider rather than the homeowner, the credit remains available to the owner of the system under 48E and can be reflected in what the customer pays. That means the structure you sell in matters more than it ever has.

We are a marketing company, not tax advisers, and this is not tax advice. Take it to your CPA and confirm it applies to how you intend to sell. But if you are building a business plan around the old residential credit, find that out now rather than in month four.

Finding 3 — Starting With Nothing Is Genuinely An Advantage
Read the rest of this week's audits and you will see why

We audit established contractors constantly. What we find is almost always the same: an ad account trained on the wrong signal, analytics installed two years late so there is no “before”, a website owned by a previous agency, reviews nobody ever asked for, and a lead vendor holding the customer relationship.

You have none of that. Nothing to undo, no contract to exit, no bad data teaching a platform the wrong thing, and no assets in somebody else's name. You can do it in the right order, once.

Right order 1. Google Business Profile — free, and it is what the map runs on 2. Analytics and conversion tracking — before the first dollar 3. A site that says what you sell and how it is structured 4. Reviews from your first customers, asked for every time 5. Only then, paid traffic

The order matters more than the budget. Doing five before one to four is how most new contractors waste their first three months of spending.

Finding 4 — Eastern Washington Is Not California
Worth being realistic about the market you are actually in

Your 509 number puts you in eastern Washington. That is a different solar market to the one most industry advice is written about, and it changes what good marketing looks like.

Two things work in your favour. Competition is far thinner than in the coastal markets, so the cost of appearing in search is lower and being visible is more achievable. And a smaller, more local market rewards reputation and word of mouth more heavily — which is exactly what a Google profile and reviews are for.

One thing does not. With the residential credit gone, the payback maths a homeowner does is longer than it was, and it is longer again in a market with lower electricity prices than California's. The honest answer is that the offer has to be built around something other than a tax credit — and working out what that is, for your market, is a better use of our first call than talking about websites.
